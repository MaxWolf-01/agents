# Attack the premise

Each failed attempt is evidence against the belief it assumed. A third attempt on the same premise spends effort where the evidence already says the answer is not, so suspect the premise, not the attempts.

- **Write the premise down.** The premise is the one sentence every failed attempt assumed.
- **Test it before the next attempt.** Find an observation that would come out differently if the premise were false, and make it. Look at how the failures are distributed, not only at how large they are. Make the check rerunnable.
- **Follow the evidence.** If it contradicts the premise, the answer is elsewhere: look for what the failures share that the premise did not predict. If it confirms the premise, ask the next "why": the earlier attempts may have treated a consequence instead of the cause.
- **Remove the condition instead of compensating for it.** If one part always lands on the losing side, change what puts it there. A mechanism that cleans up after it leaves the cause in place and adds work on every run.

The next attempt starts only once the premise is written down and tested.
