# Round 3: the page is a container, and the spec gets a page of its own

## Questions

- [Q7] **What reviews a turn's own prose?** Artifacts are reviewed inside `/mx:show` as they are built, so this covers only the headline, the questions and the details a turn record carries. It replaces Q5, which assumed a chat reply worth reviewing.
  - (a) A light review as the turn ends: today's small reviewer and its catalogue, pointed at the turn record. *my pick*
  - (b) None: the record is short and fixed in shape, and only artifacts are reviewed.
  - Why: the details and the questions are what you read first, and a small model over a few hundred words costs seconds. It is `chat_review.py` with a new input, and the rules stay in the catalogue, their one home.

## Details

- The session page only holds things: a headline, your message, the questions, these details and links. Artifacts are pages of their own that open in a new tab, so round 1 is no longer framed inside.
- A turn is a file shaped like a ticket, which every session already writes: frontmatter, a heading, fixed sections. There is no markup of its own to learn.
- The spec gets its own rendered page with its figures inside and this round's changes marked, and diffview stops carrying specs.
- The chat reply is one line and the link; you read the page once the turn has ended.
- Every artifact is built by a subagent or a fork, and I write only text files. The two pages linked below were built that way.
- The step you asked about in round 2's figure: if I answer in the chat and forget the page, the hook sends me back to move the answer onto the page.
- The `board-orients` spec retires the needs-human queue, moves every question into its ticket, and lists a ticket's sessions from `Session:` commit trailers. Neither it nor `figures-and-demos` links a spec page, since none exists yet; this feature adds that link to the feature's row and a page link to each listed session, once `board-orients` merges.
